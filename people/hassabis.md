## Known for

- Co-founder of DeepMind (2010); acquired by Google for ~£400M in 2014
- AlphaGo (2016): the first system to beat a professional Go champion (Lee Sedol 4-1)
- AlphaZero (2017): Chess, Shogi, Go from self-play without human data — from hours to superhuman
- AlphaFold 2 (2020): protein structure prediction at experimental accuracy; a 50-year open problem in biology
- Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2024 jointly with John Jumper and David Baker
- CEO of Google DeepMind since 2023 (merger of Google Brain and DeepMind)
- PhD in cognitive neuroscience (UCL, 2009): hippocampal memory systems
- Game designer in his youth: Theme Park (Bullfrog, 1994), Black & White (Elixir Studios)

## Key decisions

**HASSABIS-C001** 🟢 — AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol (Go, March 2016) through a combination of deep CNN value/policy networks + Monte Carlo Tree Search; Go was considered «unsolvable for decades» due to the state space; the victory changed the perception of what deep RL can do

**HASSABIS-C002** 🟢 — AlphaZero (2017) trained Chess, Shogi and Go from scratch via self-play without a single human example; within hours achieved superhuman level in each game; this is a demonstration that expertise can emerge from self-play alone — not from imitation of human mastery

**HASSABIS-C003** 🟢 — AlphaFold 2 (2020) achieved median GDT ~92 on CASP14 — accuracy at the level of a crystallographic experiment; DeepMind opened predictions of 200M+ proteins through the AlphaFold DB; Nobel 2024 confirmed that an AI system solved a biological problem that had been considered PhD-level work for decades

**HASSABIS-C004** 🟠 — DeepMind used games (Atari, Go, StarCraft, Quake III) as structured environments with a clear reward to develop RL algorithms that then transfer to the real world; AlphaFold is literally the application of AlphaGo-style search to structural biology; this is a deliberate methodology, not an accident

**HASSABIS-C005** 🟠 — Hassabis is one of the few top-AI leaders with a neuroscience background (hippocampal memory systems); his thesis «AI as a scientific discipline for understanding intelligence», not «AI as a set of products» shapes DeepMind as a research-driven organisation publishing in Nature/Science, not only in NeurIPS
